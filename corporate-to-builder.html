From corporate to builder: what I didn't expect

Vikram Mekala
May 2026 · 4 min read

I had a failed startup once.

Built it the traditional way — developers, designers, testers, budget, the lot. Spent months getting the right people in the room. Months more getting something built. It went nowhere. That always stayed with me.

After 25 years running technology programmes at Unilever, Shell, BP and Mars, I stepped back in 2024. Not to slow down. To build.

I wanted to understand one simple question: living in the UK, what can actually be made simpler for everyday people using AI?

But this time, I wasn't going to do it the traditional way.

Six months minimum in corporate. Three committees. One watered-down product. Then it flopped anyway.

Six days building alone. Nothing watered down.

What actually surprised me wasn't the speed. It was what I found once the layers were gone.

In corporate, the biggest bottleneck in AI isn't the technology. It's the layers. Too many teams. Too many systems of record. Too many people who need to sign something before anything moves. Everyone talks about AI transformation. Very few are actually building anything. The appetite to take risk and fail is almost zero.

The gap between having an idea and building it has collapsed. That's the story nobody in enterprise tech is telling.

I have access to Claude. I have a clear question I'm trying to answer. I have no committee between me and shipping.

Four products live now. Miru — a WhatsApp-first AI assistant for everyday life. Intel — brand and company intelligence. Space — UK space, simplified. And a plain-language AI literacy platform. All built by one person. All running on real users.

I'm still a corporate person at heart. The 25 years taught me how to think about scale, governance, stakeholder management, risk. That background makes me a better builder — I know what breaks at scale before I build it.

But I'll never go back to six committees for one idea.

The freedom — I genuinely can't put it into words. Exhilarating is the closest I've got.

Build. Discard. Improve. Ship. Repeat.

That's the whole process now.
