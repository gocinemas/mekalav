What actually brings the spark back

27 October 2026 · Vikram Mekala

I saw a post on Threads the other day. Someone asked, "People who finally found their spark back, what genuinely helped?"

It's a simple question but it stuck with me. Mostly because the answers weren't what I expected. Nobody said they found their passion or quit everything and moved to Bali. What people actually described was much smaller than that.

It came back in small pieces

When you read enough of these answers, a pattern shows up. The spark doesn't come back in one go. It comes back in bits, and usually in a certain order.

Rest, but the real kind. A lot of what feels like lost spark is just being tired. Not sleepy tired, empty tired. And scrolling doesn't fix that. It numbs it. Sleep, walks, time with nothing coming in, that's what actually refills the tank.

Moving before feeling like it. Cooking, walking, the gym, gardening. People kept saying they didn't wait to feel motivated. They moved first and the motivation followed. It's the opposite of how we usually think it works.

Making something for no reason. A sketch, a bit of writing, a small app nobody asked for. No audience, no plan to sell it. Play seems to bring the spark back faster than goals do.

Being around people who light up. Energy rubs off. One good conversation with someone who's excited about what they do can do more than weeks of thinking about it on your own.

Following small curiosities. Not "what's my purpose" but "what am I a bit interested in today?" Then actually following it for an hour. Big questions tend to freeze people. Small ones get them moving.

The one that stuck with me

One idea kept coming up in different words. People lost their spark when everything started to feel like it had to count.

Every project had to be a proof point. Every hobby had to become a side hustle. Every coffee was networking. Every week was measured against where they thought they should be by now.

When everything carries that much weight, nothing feels light anymore. And it's hard to feel a spark about something that feels heavy.

What helped wasn't lowering the stakes on everything. It was letting one thing off the hook. One thing that didn't need to lead anywhere. Funnily enough, that's often where the spark came back first, and then it spread.

Notice what's taking it

The last thing people mentioned was quieter. Sometimes the spark doesn't need finding. It just needs to stop leaking out.

A job that doesn't fit anymore. A person who always leaves you a bit flatter. A story you keep telling yourself about where you should be. Once people noticed what was draining them and stopped feeding it, the spark often came back on its own.

I don't think there's a formula here. But if you're in a flat patch right now, maybe start small. Rest properly. Make something for fun. Talk to someone who's excited about something. Let one thing not matter.

And if you've been through this and found your way back, I'd genuinely like to know what helped you.
