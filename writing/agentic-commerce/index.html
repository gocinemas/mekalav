Your next customer might be an AI

17 November 2026 · Vikram Mekala

Agentic commerce sounds like jargon. The idea is simple.

You tell an AI what you want. It finds the options, compares them and, if you let it, buys the thing. The shopper is still a person. The one doing the clicking isn't.

At Unilever I worked on pricing and early eCommerce data, including Amazon. Back then the fight was over the digital shelf: how your product showed up when a person searched. This is the next shelf. Except the one reading it is an agent.

The numbers worth knowing

It's already changing how people shop. 68% of US consumers used at least one AI tool while shopping in the past three months, according to ICSC and McKinsey.

AI shoppers are better customers. In September 2026, visits that came from AI tools to US retail sites converted 90% better than other traffic and brought in 83% more revenue per visit (Adobe). That's a big flip. In March 2025, AI traffic converted 38% worse.

It's growing fast. AI traffic to US retail sites is up 1,772% since October 2024. Over last year's holiday season it was up 693%.

But it's still small. AI platforms will be about 1.5% of US online retail sales in 2026, around $20.6 billion (EMARKETER). Nearly four times last year, but a long way from taking over.

The forecasts are huge, and all over the place. McKinsey says AI agents could handle $3 to $5 trillion of global consumer commerce by 2030. Bain says $300 to $500 billion in the US. Morgan Stanley's base case is around $190 billion. Same idea, different definitions. Read the range, not one number.

The bit nobody puts on a slide

OpenAI launched Instant Checkout in ChatGPT in September 2025, so you could buy without leaving the chat. By March 2026 it was shutting it down.

Why? Merchants didn't sign up. Forrester reckons only about 30 Shopify merchants were using it by February. ChatGPT needed live stock, delivery times and shipping costs, and scraping websites wasn't enough. Checkout now goes back to the retailer's own site.

So the agent is great at finding and comparing. The buying, the returns and the customer relationship still sit with the shop. For now.

Where the money is going

The payment companies are getting ready anyway.

Visa launched Agentic Ready in Europe, starting with the UK, with Barclays, HSBC UK, Nationwide and Revolut testing payments started by AI agents. Mastercard's Agent Pay makes agents register and get verified before they can pay for anyone, and the shopper decides what the agent is allowed to buy.

That's the real signal. Banks don't build plumbing for fads.

What to do about it

If you sell things:

  • Make your products readable by machines. Clear names, specs, sizes, price, stock and delivery times. If an agent can't read it, you don't exist in the answer.
  • Keep your checkout. Agents will send you the customer. Make the last step on your own site quick and painless.
  • Track AI traffic on its own. Split it out in your analytics. It behaves differently, and it's growing faster than anything else.
  • Start with one category. Pick the products people research hardest and fix those listings first.

If you're the shopper:

  • Let AI do the research and comparing. That's what it's good at today.
  • If you let it pay, set limits: a spending cap, approved shops, a confirmation step.
  • Check the returns policy before you buy. The agent won't be the one standing in the queue.

If you work in a big company:

  • Ask who owns this. It sits between eCommerce, marketing, IT and payments, so it's easy for it to belong to nobody.

The shop window is moving into the chat. The till isn't. Not yet.
