What Day 0 actually feels like

Vikram Mekala
May 2026 · 4 min read

Everyone talks about the programme. The governance framework, the RAID log, the steering committee, the workstream leads. Nobody talks about the moment it either works or doesn't.

That moment is Day 0.

I was the Day 0 Hypercare Lead on the Magnum Ice Cream separation from Unilever. A global company. Multiple time zones. Calls twice, sometimes three times a day for 100 days.

The incidents coming in weren't strategic. They were brutally human.

Someone didn't get paid.

A freezing cabinet couldn't be procured.

An invoice didn't generate.

Someone lost access to systems they needed that morning.

Someone got overpaid.

None of this is in the strategy deck. All of it lands on Day 0.

Underneath all of it — 14 production systems cutting over simultaneously. SAP access segregating in real time. Senior leadership on every call expecting answers.

The complexity isn't technical. It's human. Hundreds of people whose working lives are being split in two, and they just need things to work.

What most M&A programmes get wrong is they optimise for the planning phase and underinvest in the hypercare phase. The planning matters enormously. But Day 0 is where the plan meets reality, and reality always has notes.

The organisations that get through it cleanly share the same three things.

Every decision pre-made. When an incident hits at 2am you don't want a committee. You want a decision tree that already exists. Who owns this? What's the escalation path? What constitutes a critical failure vs a manageable issue? All of this needs to be decided before Day 0, not during it.
One person owns each thread. Confusion kills speed. The moment two people think they own the same incident, it slows down. Clear ownership, documented in advance, for every workstream and every class of issue.
Silence is a red flag. In hypercare, no news is not good news. If a team goes quiet, something is wrong. Either they're overwhelmed, or they're sitting on a problem they haven't escalated yet. Follow the silence every time.

Zero outages. 99%+ SLA held across 100 days.

Nerve-wracking. Chaotic. Exhausting.

One of the best experiences of my career.

If you're about to go through a separation — the technology will be fine. It's the human side that needs the plan.
