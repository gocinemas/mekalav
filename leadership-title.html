A title does not make you a leader

Vikram Mekala
May 2026 · 3 min read

But somewhere along the way, organisations forgot that.

The loudest voice gets promoted. The best politician gets the senior role. The person who says the right thing in the right room gets the big job.

And then the transformation programme fails. And nobody can explain why.

I can.

The system selected for the wrong thing.

Real leadership in a transformation is specific. Do you take the blame when things go wrong or let it roll downhill? Do you create space for your team to think or close it down? Do you back people when they take a risk, or protect yourself when it does not pay off?

Do you actually understand the people you are working with? Different cultures, different contexts, different ways of thinking. That is a learnt skill. Not everyone has it. Not everyone bothers.

In 25 years across programmes, projects and product delivery, the answer has been the same. People first. Not technology. Not process. Not the word you just added to your LinkedIn headline.

I have seen programmes with perfect technology fail because nobody in the room had the trust of the team. And I have seen underfunded, understaffed deliveries held together by one person who knew how to read a room and back their people at the right moment.

That is not soft skills. That is the job.

There is a real difference between having the title and having actually delivered it.

The organisations that get this right have better outcomes. They also have teams that want to be there.

The title comes after. Not before.
